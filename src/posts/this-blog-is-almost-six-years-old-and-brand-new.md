This blog was born in December 2020, in the middle of a pandemic, with three articles in a little over a week, and they already said a lot about me. One was about [Foveon](/post/sigma-sd-quattro-hands-on.html) sensors, my obsession back then. One was about [Contax](/post/contax-g-35mm-planar-an-undeserved-bad-reputation.html). The third was about which camera to take out in the only real [snowfall](/post/nikonos-iv-a-the-perfect-winter-camera.html) Milan has seen this decade. I had no plan. I had a lot of cameras, even more free evenings thanks to the pandemic, and the feeling that some of these objects deserved better than a forum thread. What I did not have was the faintest idea of how to build a website, so a friend built it with me. At the time, doing it alone was simply impossible.

Six years later I went back to the numbers, and I have to admit they surprised me.

![Bar chart of readers per month on this blog, December 2020 to November 2026: growth in 2021, a peak of almost 1,400 in January 2022, a sudden collapse on 23 June 2023](/img/blog-readers-by-month.png)
*Readers per month. The red bar is June 2023, the month of the patatrac.*

In 2021 I wrote twenty-five articles: medium format, Foveon sensors, Konica, Pentax 6x7, a lot of film. Readers grew slowly, from about 150 a month to almost 300, which for a blog written in the evenings from Milan already felt like a crowd. Then, at the turn of 2022, I [unboxed](/post/unboxing-a-contax-645-kit.html) a Contax 645 I had been dreaming of for years: one of the original launch kits from 1999, still intact, every piece in its own box, a camera that cost $4,000 when it came out. A few weeks later I started shooting with a [digital camera](/post/contax-tvs-digital-shooting-with-a-2002-digital-camera.html) from 2002, the Contax TVS Digital. In January almost 1,400 people read this blog. It never really came down for the rest of the year: about 12,000 readers in 2022.

In total, nearly 19,000 people from 134 countries and more than 3,900 cities, for over 30,000 pages read. Most of them came from the United States, then the United Kingdom, Italy, India, Canada and Australia. The city with the most readers was London, not Milan, which had to settle for second place just ahead of New York. And somewhere out there one person in Mongolia, one in the Marshall Islands, one in Cuba and one in Saint-Pierre-et-Miquelon read about old cameras. I would really like to know which article.

![World map where every country with at least one reader of this blog is filled with its flag](/img/blog-readers-flags.png)
*Every flag is a country where at least one person read this blog: 134 of them.*

Not that I had planned it, but the Contax took over. Ten articles about Contax cameras collected more than half of all the article views on this blog, and the two about the TVS Digital alone were read more than 5,000 times. The second one, about using it as my only [compact camera](/post/can-a-contax-tvs-digital-be-your-only-point-shoot.html), is still the most read thing I have ever written, and "contax tvs digital" was the search that brought people here more than any other. For a couple of years, if you were trying to figure out a Contax, there was a good chance you ended up here. I am not going to pretend I didn't enjoy it.

The article I am proudest of, though, is the one about converting an [old G1](/post/contax-g1-silver-label-conversion-to-green-label.html) so it could take the newer lenses. Nobody offered that service any more, so I pieced the procedure together from a repair manual and old forum posts, before it got lost in the maze of the web. It was my friend Andrea who really made it possible: thanks again, Andrea. I saved something that would otherwise have disappeared, and I still feel good about it.

Then, on 23 June 2023, *patatrac*. That is what we say in Italian when something comes crashing down all at once, and I can't think of a better word for it. From one day to the next, readers went from about twenty a day to almost nothing. Not over a season. In a day. The same friend who had helped me build the site changed something, with the best intentions, and Google stopped trusting it. When Google stops trusting a blog, it stops sending readers, and everything else follows. For the next two years this place had a few dozen visitors a month, and by the end of 2025 most of them were robots. I got burned, and I stopped looking at it. Still, I owe that friend a big thank you: without him, there would have been nothing to lose.

In 2026 I decided to take it back, and this time to build it myself. Six years ago that was out of reach. Today, with AI, someone with more ideas than technical skills can put together a website of his own, and that is what happened here. I now write about AI as well, and about the personal projects that came out of it. This site has become my small digital garden.

![The home page of the new blog: a section drawing of a house, from the greenhouse on the roof to the darkroom in the basement](/img/blog-new-house.jpg)
*The new home page: the whole blog drawn as a house, from the greenhouse on the roof to the darkroom.*

The blog is part of a house now. The old articles live in the [workshop](/workshop) and in the [writing room](/writing), each one at the same address it always had, while [the other floors](/projects) hold what I have been building in the meantime, from [Milano in dati](https://milanoindati.it/), Milan told through open data, to [Grand Strategy](https://grandstrategy.davidecaniatti.com/), an atlas of how the world's actors deal with each other, to the [Voynichizer](https://voynichizer.com/), which turns any text into a Voynich-like manuscript. [My letters on Substack](https://substack.com/@davidecaniatti), from [Milano da Bere](https://davidecaniatti96.substack.com) to [Ariosto Daily](https://ariostodaily.substack.com), have [a room of their own](/letters). The idea is simple: what I write and what I build should talk to each other, and you should be able to wander from one to the other.

This does not mean the blog becomes something else: in the workshop there will always be personal posts and art.

So this is a recap, but also an invitation. If you came here years ago for a Contax, welcome back. If you are new, start from the [latest articles](/post), or just open the door and walk around. Make yourselves comfortable, you are my guests!
